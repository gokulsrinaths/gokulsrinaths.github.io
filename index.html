I am a computer science graduate student at California State Polytechnic University, Pomona, applying to PhD programs in natural language processing and trustworthy machine learning.

My research asks a simple question: when do large language models look right while quietly being wrong? I study failures that standard checks miss. In Citation Drift I showed that references mutate, disappear, or get fabricated across multi-turn conversations, and proposed metrics and a benchmark for reference stability. In The Validity–Fidelity Gap I showed that structured-extraction pipelines can keep perfect JSON validity while extraction fidelity collapses under redundant context, an effect that is large at 8B scale and vanishes at 70B. I am also interested in cross-modal generation, including forecasting time series directly from natural-language descriptions (Zero-to-Forecast, NeurIPS 2025 workshop).

Broader interests: LLM evaluation and reproducibility, interpretability, reasoning, multi-agent systems, and fairness in AI.

Outside of research I build things. I led a multi-agent chat simulator at the Nous RL Environments Hackathon and was on the third-place team at Cal Poly Pomona’s 2025 AI for a Better Future hackathon with an AI firewall for OTP scam prevention.

selected publications

  1. AACL
    The Validity–Fidelity Gap: A Controlled Study of Representation Drift in Structured Extraction Under Redundant Contexts
    Gokul Srinath Seetha Ram, Anirudh Vasudevan, and Rashmi Elavazhagan
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: IJCNLP-AACL 2026, Nov 2026
    Abs DOI HTML PDF

    Pipelines that use LLMs for structured extraction are commonly monitored by checking that each output parses as JSON and matches the expected keys. We show that this check can stay perfect while the extracted content degrades. We call the failure representation drift: the model still finds an entity but writes it in a different form than the intended one, for example a nested key-value object where a plain string is expected. We study this with synthetic clinical notes repeated 1x-32x. For LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, micro-F1 falls from 0.499 to 0.259 while JSON validity stays 1.00. We introduce the Differential Redundancy Effect (DRE), the extra F1 drop under repeated content relative to a length-matched unrelated-filler control. The effect is large at 8B and near zero at 70B and Qwen2.5-72B. We also give a zero-ground-truth drift check and monitoring recommendations.

  2. WASP
    Citation Drift: Measuring Reference Stability in Multi-Turn LLM Conversations
    Gokul Srinath Seetha Ram
    In Proceedings of the Third Workshop for Artificial Intelligence for Scientific Publications (WASP), Dec 2025
    Abs DOI HTML PDF

    Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used for scientific writing and research assistance, yet their ability to maintain consistent citations across multi-turn conversations remains largely unexplored. This study introduces the concept of citation drift, the phenomenon where references mutate, disappear, or get fabricated during extended LLM interactions. Through an analysis of 240 conversations across 4 LLaMA models using 36 authentic scientific papers from 6 domains, this work demonstrates significant citation instability. Citation stability varies dramatically across models, with llama-4-maverick-17b showing the highest stability (0.481) and llama-4-scout-17b showing the worst fabrication rates (0.856). The study introduces novel metrics including citation drift entropy and willingness-to-cite, providing a framework for evaluating LLM citation reliability in scientific contexts.

  3. NeurIPS WS
    Zero-to-Forecast: Natural Language to Time Series Prediction via Cross-Modal Ensembles
    Gokul Srinath Seetha Ram
    In NeurIPS 2025 Workshop on Recent Advances in Time Series Foundation Models: Have We Reached the BERT Moment?, Dec 2025
    Abs HTML PDF

    Zero-to-Forecast is a cross-modal framework that turns natural-language descriptions into numerical time series forecasts without historical data. It combines LLM reasoning with domain- and pattern-specific predictors and post-hoc calibration (domain-aware smoothing, monotonic constraints). On the NL2TS-675 benchmark spanning six domains, the domain-optimized ensemble reaches an overall MAE of 16.06, with every domain under 25 MAE. Code, artifacts, and a live interactive demo are released.

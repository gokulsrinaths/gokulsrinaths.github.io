I am a computer science graduate student at California State Polytechnic University, Pomona, applying to PhD programs in natural language processing and trustworthy machine learning.

My research asks a simple question: **when do large language models look right while quietly being wrong?** I study failures that standard checks miss. In [Citation Drift](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wasp-main.20/) I showed that references mutate, disappear, or get fabricated across multi-turn conversations, and proposed metrics and a benchmark for reference stability. In [The Validity–Fidelity Gap](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-aacl.107/) I showed that structured-extraction pipelines can keep perfect JSON validity while extraction fidelity collapses under redundant context, an effect that is large at 8B scale and vanishes at 70B. I am also interested in cross-modal generation, including forecasting time series directly from natural-language descriptions ([Zero-to-Forecast](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/130445), NeurIPS 2025 workshop).

Broader interests: LLM evaluation and reproducibility, interpretability, reasoning, multi-agent systems, and fairness in AI.

Outside of research I build things. I led a multi-agent chat simulator at the Nous RL Environments Hackathon and was on the third-place team at Cal Poly Pomona's 2025 AI for a Better Future hackathon with an AI firewall for OTP scam prevention.
